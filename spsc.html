Low-latency IPC case study

SPSC

A compact, robust message pipeline designed around fairness, isolation, and predictable latency under real system pressure.

Why the design was needed

The problem

The challenge was to support IPC that felt fair between processes without trading away reliability. MPSC queues and sockets were rejected because they introduced more coordination overhead and less predictable behavior for the intended workload.

How the solution was built

What I did

I owned the serialization scope, clarified the full/empty ambiguity, and weighed the sacrificial-slot tradeoff before settling on a 64-slot sizing decision that balanced simplicity and throughput. The result was a path that was explicit rather than clever.

What the result revealed

What came of it

The system remained safe, isolated, and fast while still being understandable. The main remaining open question is whether the sleep-path cost can be reduced further without giving up the calm and predictable behavior that made the design attractive in the first place.
